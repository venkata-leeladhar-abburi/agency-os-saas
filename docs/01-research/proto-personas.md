# Proto-personas

**Status: hypotheses, not validated.** These are built from desk research and One Stop Solutions' own experience running an agency. They have no invented quotes or statistics. Replace them with research-backed personas after the interviews (`research-plan.md`). Names are illustrative.

Six primary personas, two per side of the relationship, plus one secondary.

---

## P1. Kiran: studio owner, India

- **Profile:** runs a 9-person design and development studio in a tier-2 city. Clients are mostly Indian SMBs, plus two in the UAE and UK.
- **Today's tools (hypothesis):** WhatsApp, Google Sheets for leads, Google Docs or Canva for proposals, Notion or Trello for tasks, an invoicing app or Excel, Razorpay links, Google Drive for handoff.
- **Jobs:** win steady work without paying marketplace commissions; look as professional as a big agency; know which projects make money; stop being the bottleneck.
- **Pains:**
  - leads scattered across DMs and calls
  - proposals rewritten each time
  - clients asking "what's the status?"
  - scope changes agreed on WhatsApp and never billed
  - chasing payments
  - no single view of team load
- **Success looks like:** a lead becomes a signed client in a day; the client watches progress without calling; invoices go out on time; profit per project is known.
- **Portals:** Agency owner (desktop at the office, phone on the move).
- **Design implications:** Today view with dues and follow-ups; one-click lead to client; change requests that become quotes; WhatsApp sharing; INR and GST by default.

## P2. Ananya: solo freelancer, India, works with foreign clients

- **Profile:** a UI/UX designer in a metro city. Leads come from Instagram, Behance, LinkedIn and referrals. Half her clients are abroad (US and UK startups).
- **Today's tools (hypothesis):** Notion, Figma, Google Docs, PayPal or Wise, Excel invoices; not registered for GST, or recently registered.
- **Jobs:** look credible next to agencies; get paid in USD without losing much to fees; protect her time from scope creep; keep the paperwork right.
- **Pains:**
  - writing contracts and proposals from scratch
  - time-zone juggling
  - unclear export-invoice rules (LUT, FIRA)
  - clients disappearing before final payment
  - portfolio scattered across sites
- **Success looks like:** a professional profile that shows how she works; a proposal and contract sent in minutes; correct export invoices; paid before handoff.
- **Portals:** Solo workspace.
- **Design implications:** a Solo plan without team layers; templates from the Playbook; international client mode (dual time zones, currency); a handoff package tied to final payment.

## P3. Arjun: project lead or developer, India

- **Profile:** full-stack developer and project lead in a 15-person agency.
- **Jobs:** know exactly what to build; get approvals without chasing; hand over cleanly.
- **Pains:** requirements in chat threads; unclear approval of designs; daily update rituals; logins and credentials passed around in messages; last-minute "small changes".
- **Success looks like:** a clear PRD; a gate approval recorded in one place; a checklist that makes handoff predictable.
- **Portals:** Project lead and Team member (desktop for work, phone for check-in and updates).
- **Design implications:** My day view; daily update in under a minute; gate approval requests; a handoff checklist; credentials only in the vault.

## P4. Srinivas: SMB owner, client in India

- **Profile:** in his 50s, owner of a family retail and manufacturing business; uses a smartphone daily; most comfortable in Telugu; WhatsApp is his main tool. Commissioning his first ERP or website.
- **Jobs:** get the system built on budget; understand what he's paying for; approve quickly; avoid surprises.
- **Pains:** technical words; long documents; not knowing what happens next; fear of hidden costs; logging into many tools.
- **Success looks like:** one link, opened from WhatsApp, showing progress, what needs him and what he owes; pay by UPI in two taps.
- **Portals:** Client portal (phone).
- **Design implications:** a mobile-first portal; plain language (Telugu and Hindi later); large tap targets; one "Needs you" list; UPI payment; no forced password on first visit (magic link, then set a password).

## P5. Sarah: agency owner, international

- **Profile:** owns a 15-person digital agency in London or Dubai; works with UK and EU clients; partners with Indian developers.
- **Today's tools (hypothesis):** Slack, HubSpot, Xero, Stripe, a PSA or project tool, Google Workspace.
- **Jobs:** grow margin; delight clients with clear reporting; stay compliant (GDPR, contracts); reduce tool sprawl.
- **Pains:** paying per seat for many tools; portals that look generic; weekly status reports written by hand; handoff gaps leading to support tickets.
- **Success looks like:** branded portal on her domain; status reports drafted automatically; profit per project in real time.
- **Portals:** Agency owner.
- **Design implications:** a polished desktop experience; portal on a custom domain; GDPR tools (DPA, data export); integrations (Slack, Xero) on the roadmap; USD/GBP pricing.

## P6. Mike: startup founder, client abroad hiring an Indian agency

- **Profile:** a seed-stage US founder hiring an Indian agency for an MVP.
- **Jobs:** find a trustworthy team; protect IP; see progress across time zones; receive everything needed to run the product after launch.
- **Pains:** trust in a team he has never met; vague timelines; time-zone gaps; incomplete handoff (missing credentials, docs, repo access).
- **Success looks like:** verified reviews and a clear process before signing; NDA and IP terms signed; weekly updates in his time zone; a complete handoff with an acceptance certificate.
- **Portals:** Hirer account, then Client portal (desktop and iPhone).
- **Design implications:** process and toolkit on the agency profile; dual time zones; weekly status reports; the handoff package; USD invoices and card payments.

## Secondary: Meera, HR and operations

Runs attendance, leave, payslips and contractor payouts for a 25-person agency. Needs approvals in one place and monthly records that match payroll. Uses the HR portal on desktop and approves leave on a phone.
